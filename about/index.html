A note, not a résumé

I’m still early in my career, and I would rather be precise than impressive.

Curiosity is the through-line.

I move between interests because they feed each other. Systems thinking helps with backend design. Games teach feedback and pacing. Music and visual art make interfaces feel less mechanical. Space and futuristic technology keep the questions big.

My public projects reflect that range: a Windows utility that prioritises safe file renames, a private habit tracker, an Android creator workspace, a campus reporting workflow, a 3D browser game, and a full local library system. They are not all the same kind of product, and that is the point.

I care about honest documentation. If a feature is planned, it should be labelled as planned. If a release has not been verified, that should be clear too. Good engineering includes telling the truth about what exists.

How I learn

A loop, not a ladder.

I do not wait until I know everything before starting. I build a small version, pay attention to what breaks, and use that to decide what to learn next.

  1. 01

    Notice

    Start with a real friction, an odd question, or a detail that feels slightly wrong.

  2. 02

    Break it down

    Separate the problem into inputs, decisions, edge cases, and the smallest useful version.

  3. 03

    Build

    Make something concrete enough to test — a screen, a workflow, a game loop, or a public README.

  4. 04

    Reflect

    Read the rough edges, document what is true, and improve the next iteration.
